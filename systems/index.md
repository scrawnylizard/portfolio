A system is a set of parts, connected so that together they produce a behavior the parts can't produce alone. Most of the problems in my life aren't caused by one broken part. They come from the way the parts interact: things that pile up, feedback that comes too late, and fixes that make things worse a few months later.

This section is a practice space. Each **domain** (Computing, then others) holds a handful of **systems**. Every system gets the same model:

- **Purpose**: what the system actually does, judged by its behavior rather than its stated goal.
- **Boundary**: what's inside the system and what counts as its environment.
- **Stocks and flows**: what accumulates, and what fills and drains it.
- **Feedback loops**: *reinforcing* loops (R) that amplify change and *balancing* loops (B) that resist it.
- **Leverage points**: where to intervene, ranked using Donella Meadows' [twelve places to intervene in a system](https://donellameadows.org/archives/leverage-points-places-to-intervene-in-a-system/).

The practice is a cycle: **model → observe → experiment → log → revise the model.** Each system keeps a dated log of what I noticed, what I tried, and what happened.
